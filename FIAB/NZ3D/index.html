About, data and limitations

Model. BRIS-NZ: MET Norway's BRIS (anemoi, 2.5 km limited area inside a global N320 grid) moved to a New Zealand Lambert grid and fine-tuned on IFS analyses 2017–2025 with rollout; the global part is driven by the AIFS forecast (all-AI chain) and the 2.5 km surface height is the cell mean of the IFS TCo7999 climate. 72 h from IFS analyses at 00 and 12 UTC, output every 6 h.

Hourly frames are interpolated from the 6-hourly data: temperature, dewpoint and snow level with a monotone cubic Hermite curve, wind and cloud linearly; precipitation is spread over each 6 h window so that the hourly amounts add up exactly to the 6 h total. Hour h shows the precipitation of the hour ending at h.

Rain or snow. The share of snow comes from the height of the model cell against the snow level (BRIS has no snowfall output). Snow level is the height of the 0 °C wet-bulb temperature, from the 2 m temperature and dewpoint at the model surface and the temperature and humidity on the pressure levels above it (1000–500 hPa). Amounts are water equivalent, not snow depth.

Terrain is the IFS TCo7999 climate orography (about 1.3 km; the whole-domain view is coarser, the regions under “Explore” are at about 1.25 km), vertically exaggerated. The weather is on the model cells (2.5 km), so the relief and the snow line painted on it are finer than the weather. Snow is painted where the terrain is above the snow level of the hour. Wind trails show the 10 m wind over the surface, not three-dimensional trajectories.
