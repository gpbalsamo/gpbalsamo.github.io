About, data and limitations

Model. A 6-arc-minute (0.1°, about 10 km) limited area over 66–142°E, 9–55°N on the CaMa-Flood glb_06min cells inside a global N320 grid, warm-started from BRIS-NZ (MET Norway's BRIS translated to New Zealand) and trained on IFS operational analyses 2016–2026 (single step, then rollout to 24 h); the global part is driven by the AIFS forecast. 72 h from the IFS analysis, output every 6 h; a 72 h forecast takes about half a minute on one GPU.

Hourly frames are interpolated from the 6-hourly data: temperature, dewpoint and snow level with a monotone cubic Hermite curve, wind and cloud linearly; precipitation is spread over each 6 h window so that the hourly amounts add up exactly to the 6 h total. Hour h shows the precipitation of the hour ending at h.

Rain or snow. The share of snow comes from the height of the model cell against the snow level. Snow level is the height of the 0 °C wet-bulb temperature, from the 2 m temperature and dewpoint at the model surface and the temperature and humidity on the pressure levels above it (1000–500 hPa). Amounts are water equivalent, not snow depth.

3D cloud is drawn in the browser from the model's relative humidity on its pressure levels (1000–300 hPa): cloud where the humidity passes a threshold that rises from 60 % at 300 hPa to 90 % at 1000 hPa (the slider scales it), each level at its standard-atmosphere height, the volume ray-marched over the exaggerated terrain. It is an illustration of where the air is near saturation, not the model's cloud cover (that is the readout and the meteogram). Isobars are the mean sea-level pressure, smoothed, faded over terrain above ~1500 m where sea-level pressure is an extrapolation. The text forecast is written in the browser by fixed rules from the point's hourly values, not by a forecaster.

Terrain is the IFS TCo7999 climate orography (about 1.3 km; the whole-domain view is coarser, the regions under “Explore” are at about 1.25 km), vertically exaggerated. The weather is on the model cells (0.1°, about 9.4 × 11 km), so the relief and the snow line painted on it are finer than the weather. Snow is painted where the terrain is above the snow level of the hour. Wind trails show the 10 m wind over the surface, not three-dimensional trajectories.
